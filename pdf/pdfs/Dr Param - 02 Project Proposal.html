01 A Note Before You Read This

Thank you for sharing the vision for the platform. What you're describing — a Sesame/Zocdoc/Klarity-style experience for patients, backed by an OpenLoop-style clinical network — is a well-understood category, but it's genuinely three products in one: a patient app, a clinician workspace, and an internal operations console.

The clinician and ops sides are usually more complex to build than the patient-facing app, and they're also where most telehealth projects quietly blow their budget and timeline. This proposal lays out how we'd build this in three versions — a lean, real, prescribing V1 you can put in front of patients in about 17 weeks, followed by V2 and V3 that add insurance billing, scale, and the heavier compliance features once V1 has proven the model.

02 Three Connected Products
ProductWho Uses ItWhat It Does
Patient AppThe end patientDiscovery, sign-up, intake, booking, the visit itself, prescriptions/care plan, and payment.
Provider DashboardContracted/employed cliniciansCase queue, video visits, charting, e-prescribing, schedule and earnings.
Admin ConsoleDr. Param's ops teamOversight of every patient and provider, approvals, compliance, reporting, and refunds.

All three share one underlying patient/provider/visit data model — built once, used three ways — rather than three disconnected systems stitched together later.

05 How We'll Work
  • Compliance-first architecture — every PHI-touching service runs behind a signed BAA from week one, even in the lean V1.
  • Data-driven configuration — service lines, intake questionnaires, and state coverage are configured, not hard-coded.
  • Weekly demo + async standup updates — you see working software every week, not a status report.
  • Staging environment with synthetic data for your team to test against before every release.
  • Formal UAT window before go-live, with a defined bug-severity triage process.
